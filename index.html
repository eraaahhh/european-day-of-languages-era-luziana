Celebrate linguistic diversity

The European Day of Languages is celebrated each year on 26 September. It was established in 2001 at the joint initiative of the Council of Europe and the European Commission.

Why this day matters

Through this action, some 700 million citizens across the Council of Europe’s 46 member states are encouraged to discover and learn languages at any age, whether within formal education or alongside it. This reflects the shared conviction of the Council of Europe and the European Commission that linguistic diversity is a powerful means of fostering intercultural understanding and a fundamental component of Europe’s rich cultural heritage.

Languages as bridges

Learning languages helps people connect across cultures and build mutual respect.

Learning at any age

Language learning can happen in schools, families, workplaces, and everyday life.

Europe’s heritage

Linguistic diversity is part of Europe’s identity and a source of cultural richness.
